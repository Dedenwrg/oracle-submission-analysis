Oracle Submission Data Analysis Report

1 Executive Summary

This report documents trends in Autonity Oracle submissions from December 2024 through June 2025. The analysis covers seven consecutive months of validator performance data across ten distinct issue areas.

1.1 Overview of Issues Analyzed

The analysis covered ten distinct issue areas:

  1. Missing or Null Submissions: Examining validators that failed to submit price data
  2. Irregular Submission Frequency: Analyzing abnormal timing patterns in submissions
  3. Out-of-Range Values: Detecting suspicious price values compared to benchmarks
  4. Stale/Lagging Data: Identifying validators that fail to update prices when markets move
  5. Confidence Value Anomalies: Examining issues with confidence metrics
  6. Cross-Rate Inconsistency: Assessing mathematical consistency across token prices
  7. Timing/Synchronization Issues: Analyzing timestamp disparities between validators
  8. Weekend Effects: Investigating behavior during market closures
  9. Vendor Downtime: Detecting submission stoppages
  10. Security/Malicious Behavior: Looking for potential manipulation patterns

The analysis presents quantitative metrics for each issue area in a month-over-month format.

6.2 Key Findings

  1. Validator Participation Decline: The number of completely inactive validators increased from 4 (~7.5% of the committee) to 9 (20% of the committee) over the seven-month period, with submission completeness dropping from 91.5% to 18.9%.

  2. Data Quality Trends: While out-of-range values and cross-rate consistency remained stable, other metrics showed signs of weakening, particularly in missing submissions, timing synchronization, and weekend effects.

  3. Coordination Patterns: The emergence and growth of coordinated submission groups (from 0 to 5 groups involving 20 validators) could reduce the independence of price discovery.

  4. Infrastructure Reliability Trends: Vendor downtime events became more frequent and involved larger validator sets, with the largest outage growing from no major events in December to 120-minute outages affecting 31+ validators by June.

6.3 Potential Mitigation Options

  1. Validator Replacement: With 81% of submission slots incomplete by June 2025, replacement of inactive validators with vetted candidates may be considered, subject to governance preferences and risk appetite.

  2. Performance-Based Incentive Restructuring: Consider implementing rewards that more strongly favor high-quality, independent submissions.

  3. Enhanced Monitoring Infrastructure: Explore deploying real-time dashboards with automated alerting for deviations.

  4. Weekend Policy Clarification: Clarify and document guidelines for handling FX data during market closures.

  5. Validator Quality Scoring System: Consider adopting a transparent scoring framework weighted 40% uptime, 30% accuracy, 30% consistency.

7 Conclusion

The seven-month analysis period from December 2024 to June 2025 suggests a noticeable deterioration in several Oracle data quality dimensions. While some metrics like out-of-range values and cross-rate consistency remained stable, material issues emerged in validator participation, data freshness, and system coordination.

The progression from an “Excellent” overall rating in December 2024 to “Poor” by June 2025 suggests systemic issues that may warrant focused attention from protocol governance and infrastructure operators. The Oracle’s ability to provide reliable pricing for the Autonity ecosystem appears compromised in several respects, and further work may be required to restore functionality and trust to previous levels.

Potential areas of focus include: - Evaluating options for replacing inactive validators - Reducing the influence of coordination clusters - Strengthening quality-oriented incentives - Enhancing real-time monitoring capabilities - Establishing clearer operational policies

If left unaddressed, these trends could affect the Oracle system’s ability to provide accurate, timely, and independent price discovery for the Autonity network.

7.1 Next Steps

The findings from this testnet analysis provide a foundation for further investigation and action. The recommended next steps are:

  • Re-run the analysis on Mainnet: Verify the extent to which the issues identified in testnet are present on mainnet. This will help determine whether the observed patterns are specific to the testnet environment or reflect broader systemic concerns.
  • Review focus areas with stakeholders: Engage with the validator community and protocol development team to discuss the identified issues, validate the findings, and collaboratively develop appropriate responses and improvements to the Oracle system.

The analysis has been run on October 2025 Mainnet data. Compared to the Tiber Challenge months, mainnet October has proven substantially better on reliability, accuracy and robustness. This moves the reported oracle data quality from an overall “Poor” rating on Testnet to a “Good” rating on Mainnet. Review of focus areas with stakeholders is to do.
